One important improvement before we train

There is a major methodological issue I want us to solve before the model is finalized.

Your data spans June 21, 2023 through September 7, 2026, and the dataset contains both loan dates and repayment/outcome information. A proper production model should ideally answer:

"Using information available at or shortly after loan origination, can we predict whether this loan will eventually become NPL?"

rather than:

"Using the current state of the loan, can we identify loans that are already NPL?"

Those are completely different ML problems.

If we do this correctly, you'll be able to put on your CV/portfolio something much stronger than "built a loan default model."

You can say:

Built an end-to-end machine learning credit-risk solution using 65K+ loan records to predict NPL risk, incorporating data cleaning, feature engineering, class-imbalance handling, model evaluation, probability-based risk scoring, and Power BI visualization.

Let's do this as an actual project, not just theory.
